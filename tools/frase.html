What Frase does

Frase is a content optimization platform that combines AI research, SEO scoring, and GEO readiness scoring in a single editor. You write or paste your content into Frase's editor and it scores your article against both traditional SEO criteria, keyword placement, heading structure, internal links, and GEO criteria, entity density, fact density, citation readiness, answer-first structure, and schema recommendations, simultaneously, in real time.

The dual scoring is the differentiator. Every other content optimization tool on the market scores for SEO only, with GEO treated as an afterthought or an add-on. Frase built the GEO scoring layer into its core product, which means operators making the SEO-to-GEO transition don't need to switch tools or layer on additional software.

The practical workflow: research your topic using Frase's SERP analysis (it pulls and summarizes the top-ranking pages for your target query), write or import your draft into the editor, and watch the dual score update in real time as you add content. The score tells you when you've hit the threshold for both SEO ranking potential and GEO citation readiness.

What the GEO scoring layer checks

Frase's GEO scoring evaluates several dimensions that traditional SEO tools don't measure. Entity density, how many specific, named entities (people, places, organizations, concepts) appear in the content, which improves the likelihood of AI engines understanding what the article is about beyond keyword matching. Fact density, how many verifiable, specific claims appear, which correlates with citation worthiness. Answer-first structure, whether the content leads with the direct answer to the implied query before providing supporting detail, which matches how AI engines extract "answer capsules" for direct citation. Citation readiness, whether claims are supported with attributable sources that engines can cross-reference.

Each of these dimensions maps directly to the mechanisms by which AI engines select content for citation. Improving these scores is not optimization for its own sake, it's making content more legible to the retrieval systems that determine whether your content gets cited.
